25 Weeks Pregnant Fetal Development

Baby Development: Baby responds to touch and sound consistently. Hair shows pigmentation. Hands are fully formed with well-developed fingerprints unique to your baby.

Pregnancy Symptoms: You may feel baby hiccuping — a rhythmic pulsing sensation. Carpal tunnel syndrome (wrist tingling) is common in pregnancy.

Weekly Tip: Write your birth plan now while you have time to research and discuss it calmly with your doctor or healthcare provider.

At 25 weeks pregnant, your baby is approximately the size of a Cauliflower (Small).

In-Depth: 25 Weeks Pregnant

Welcome to week 25! Your baby is gaining fat and muscle at a rapid pace, plumping up their previously wrinkled skin. Their capillaries are filling with blood, and their lungs are preparing for their first breath.

Fetal Development This Week

A major milestone around 25 weeks is the development of the lungs. The lungs are starting to form surfactant, a vital substance that helps the air sacs inflate and keeps them from collapsing. While they aren't ready to breathe outside the womb just yet, this is a critical step in their respiratory development.

Your baby's senses are also sharpening. Their hearing is now sophisticated enough that they can distinguish your voice from others. You might notice them reacting to loud noises with a sudden jump or increased kicking. Their nostrils, which were previously plugged shut, are beginning to open up.

Your Body at 25 Weeks

As you approach the end of the second trimester, the physical toll on your body increases. You might be experiencing Symphysis Pubis Dysfunction (SPD) or general pelvic girdle pain. This is caused by the hormone relaxin, which loosens your ligaments in preparation for birth, but can make your joints feel unstable and sore.

Clinical Tip: If you're experiencing pelvic pain, avoid heavy lifting and try to keep your knees together when getting out of the car or turning over in bed. A supportive pregnancy belt or consulting a pelvic floor physical therapist can provide significant relief.

Preparing for the Weeks Ahead

Between weeks 24 and 28, your healthcare provider will likely schedule a glucose screening test to check for gestational diabetes. It's a standard procedure to ensure your body is processing sugars correctly during pregnancy. Now is also a good time to start researching pediatricians and planning your birth preferences.
