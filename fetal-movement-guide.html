The Complete Guide to Fetal Movement Tracking

Medical & Editorial Review
Content reviewed against ACOG clinical guidelines on fetal monitoring.
Last reviewed: August 2026. This information is for educational purposes only. Always consult your healthcare provider if you have concerns about your baby's movements.

Feeling your baby move is one of the most exciting parts of pregnancy. But tracking those movements isn't just for bonding—it's a crucial clinical tool to monitor your baby's health and well-being, especially in the third trimester.

When Will I Start Feeling Movements?

Most first-time mothers begin to feel movements (often called "quickening") between 18 and 22 weeks of pregnancy. If you've been pregnant before, you might feel them as early as 16 weeks because you know what to look for.

Early movements are often described as:

  • Flutters or "butterflies"
  • Tapping or popping bubbles
  • A light swishing sensation

If you have an anterior placenta (the placenta is located on the front wall of your uterus), it acts as a cushion, which might delay when you first feel movements or make them feel less intense.

Why is Tracking Movement Important?

By the third trimester (around week 28), your baby's movements should establish a somewhat predictable pattern. A reduction in fetal movement can sometimes be an early warning sign that the baby is under stress or not receiving enough oxygen.

Monitoring kicks helps you understand what is "normal" for your baby so you can quickly identify if something changes.

How to Do a "Kick Count"

The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) recommends tracking fetal movements starting at 28 weeks (or 26 weeks if you have a high-risk pregnancy).

The "Count to 10" Method

  1. Pick the right time: Babies are often most active after you eat a meal, drink something cold, or after you've been physically active. Pick a time of day when your baby is usually active.
  2. Get comfortable: Lie on your left side (which maximizes blood flow to the uterus) or sit in a comfortable chair with your feet up.
  3. Start counting: Count every kick, roll, swish, or jab. (Hiccups do not count).
  4. Stop at 10: Note how long it takes to reach 10 distinct movements.

The Goal: You should feel 10 movements within 2 hours. Most of the time, it will take less than 30 minutes!

When to Call Your Doctor

Do not wait until your next appointment if you have concerns about your baby's movements. You should contact your healthcare provider or go to maternity triage immediately if:

  • You do not feel 10 movements within 2 hours of focused counting.
  • There is a sudden or significant decrease in your baby's normal movement pattern.
  • You haven't felt the baby move all day.

Myth Buster: It is a myth that babies move less at the end of pregnancy because they "run out of room." While the type of movement might feel different (more rolls and stretches instead of sharp kicks), the frequency should remain the same up until birth.
