The Ultimate Hospital Bag Checklist (2026 Guide)

Pack smart, not heavy. Here is the realistic, no-nonsense checklist of what you actually need for labor, delivery, and postpartum recovery.

When should you pack your hospital bag? The general rule is to have it ready by week 36 of your pregnancy. Babies arrive on their own schedule, and the last thing you want to do during early labor is scramble for your toothbrush.

Pro Tip: Pack three separate bags. One for you, one for the baby, and a smaller "labor bag" or partner bag that stays easily accessible during the birth itself.

1. The Mom Bag (Postpartum Recovery)

The hospital will provide basic mesh underwear and giant pads, but bringing your own recovery items will make your stay significantly more comfortable.

  • Comfortable robe or button-down pajamas (easy for nursing and skin-to-skin)
  • Nursing bras or comfortable sports bras (2-3)
  • Slip-on sandals or slippers with grips (your feet may be swollen)
  • Going-home outfit (maternity clothes?"your bump doesn't vanish instantly!)
  • Extra-long phone charging cable (10ft+)
  • Toiletries: Lip balm (hospitals are dry), hair ties, toothbrush, deodorant, dry shampoo

2. The Baby Bag

Hospitals provide diapers, wipes, and basic swaddles while you are there. You only need a few essentials to bring your baby home.

  • Going-home outfit (bring one newborn size and one 0-3 months size just in case)
  • Approved infant car seat (Must be installed *before* you drive to the hospital)
  • A soft blanket for the car ride home
  • Pediatrician's contact information
  • Optional: A cute swaddle/hat set if you plan to take announcement photos

Velcro Swaddle Wraps

Traditional hospital blanket swaddles are notoriously difficult to master when you're exhausted. Velcro swaddles guarantee a secure, snug fit that helps newborns sleep safely and comfortably.

3. The Partner / Labor Bag

Birth partners need to be self-sufficient so they can focus on supporting the mother. This bag should have everything they need for a 1-3 day stay.

  • High-protein snacks (Granola bars, trail mix, jerky?"hospital cafeterias close at night)
  • Reusable water bottles
  • Change of clothes and basic toiletries
  • Cash for vending machines and parking
  • Pillow and a blanket (hospital sleeping chairs are famously uncomfortable)
  • Printed copy of your birth plan and hospital registration documents

What NOT to pack

Leave your valuables (jewelry, large amounts of cash), heavy books (you likely won't read them), and too many diapers at home. The hospital will give you plenty of supplies for the baby during your stay. Take advantage of it!
